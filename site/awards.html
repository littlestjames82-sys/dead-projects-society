The 2026 Obituary Awards

Year-end honors for the famous dead of the Public Grounds.

Walk the graveyard Bury a GitHub user

Every December the Society reviews the famous graves in the Public Grounds and gives its honors. Awarded without appeal, as all Society honors are. The only appeal is resurrection.

Most Missed

Google Reader

2005 — 2013 · Killed by its own family

Thirteen years after the funeral, the mourners still gather weekly to say it should have been someone else. The Society has stopped taking this complaint. It agrees.

Gone Too Soon

Vine

2013 — 2017 · Starved by its parent

Four years, six seconds at a time. Its successor ate the world with the same recipe and none of the brevity. The Society notes the timing.

The Longest Goodbye

Internet Explorer

1995 — 2022 · Faded away quietly

Twenty-seven years. At the end it was still, by wide agreement, the browser used to download other browsers. A whole career spent being replaced, gracefully, forever.

The Most Honorable Death

Google Stadia

2019 — 2023 · Died in a pivot

The only funeral that paid the mourners: full refunds for everyone, controllers unlocked on the way out. The Society wishes more deaths were this tidy.

The Phoenix Candidate

Winamp

1997 — 2013 · Cold case

Filed as a cold case, and then in 2024 its source code walked out of the archive on its own. The Society keeps this stone loose.

The Legal Precedent

Napster

1999 — 2001 · Died in court

Dead at two, and the industry it frightened into streaming still sends no flowers. Short life, long shadow, download complete.

Best Preserved

GeoCities

1994 — 2009 · Neighborhood demolished

A million neighborhoods demolished, then rebuilt from memory by volunteer archivists. The under-construction signs turned out to be load-bearing.

The Longest Voicemail

Skype

2003 — 2025 · Died as it lived: one more call

Twenty-two years of "can you hear me now," before there was a meeting link for everything. The call has now ended. The Society has hung up, respectfully.

All twenty-four famous graves, with their causes and last words, stand in the Public Grounds. Your own grounds can be read by the Society's instrument: Dead Projects Society on GitHub. It reads git history only. Your code is never read.
